## Commands

```bash
npm run dev     # dev server at http://localhost:3000
npm run build   # production build (also the main type-check)
npm run lint    # eslint (flat config, eslint-config-next)
```

There is no test suite. `next.config.ts` allows `*.trycloudflare.com` as a dev origin so the dev server can be shared through a Cloudflare tunnel for multiplayer testing.

## What this is

**Talo**: social games to play together — in pairs, with friends, in groups or solo. The core flow is: pick a game, create a room, send the link (WhatsApp), others tap it, type a name and play; no sign-up. The home (`app/page.tsx`) shows featured games and the catalog by shelf (`CATEGORIES`), filtered "Para jugar" by `PlayContext`. Its hero has «Jugar ya» (`components/QuickPlay.tsx`, `lib/quickPlay.ts`): for someone alone, a sheet with the games that start in seconds — the one-on-one duels go through «Jugar en línea» (matchmaking, a bot if nobody's there) and the public worlds (Viborita, Ladrillazo, Minigolf, El tronco, Puentes) through their `enterOnline` — plus «Sorpréndeme»; each game's module is imported only when picked. A new duel or world goes in `QUICK_GAMES`. Each game has a lobby at `/{game}` and rooms at `/{game}/[roomId]`; games so far: `rompecabezas` (collaborative/versus jigsaw), `memotest` (pairs), `tutti` (Tutti Frutti, at `/tutti-frutti`), `dibujo` (Dibujá y adiviná), `codigo` (Código secreto, at `/codigo-secreto`), `deslizante` (sliding puzzle race), `cuatro` (4 en línea, at `/4-en-linea`), `palabra` (Palabra secreta, a Wordle race, at `/palabra-secreta`), `trivia`, `impostor` (El impostor), `quiensoy` (¿Quién soy?, at `/quien-soy`), `naval` (Batalla naval, at `/batalla-naval`), `probable` (¿Quién es más probable?, at `/quien-es-mas-probable`), `conoces` (¿Cuánto me conocés?, at `/cuanto-me-conoces`), `generala`, `emojis` (Adiviná con emojis, at `/adivina-con-emojis`), `verdades` (Dos verdades y una mentira, at `/dos-verdades-una-mentira`), `damas`, `cajas` (Puntos y cajas, at `/puntos-y-cajas`), `reto` (Verdad o reto, at `/verdad-o-reto`), `mimica`, `ahorcado`, `sopa` (Sopa de letras, at `/sopa-de-letras`) and `carreras` (2D racing on a shared screen, phones as controllers) and `carreras3d` (Carreras 3D, at `/carreras-3d`: on a shared screen or each on their own phone) and `arena` (Arena medieval, at `/arena-medieval`: 3D knights by teams, free-for-all, king of the hill or survival waves, on five maps, optional bots), `yonunca` (Yo nunca nunca, at `/yo-nunca`), `onda` (En la misma onda, at `/en-la-misma-onda`), `telefono` (Teléfono descompuesto, at `/telefono-descompuesto`), `ludo` (Parchís, at `/parchis`; `/ludo` redirects there), `lobo` (Hombre lobo, at `/hombre-lobo`), `ajedrez`, `chinchon`, `truco`, `fuego` (Fuego cruzado, at `/fuego-cruzado`: a first-person paint shooter where the players are talos with toy blasters; party modes, chaos every minute and an ability each; on each phone or on a big screen split for up to 4 with the phones as controllers), `otelo`, `backgammon`, `oca` (Juego de la Oca, at `/juego-de-la-oca`), `domino`, `pasteleria` (a 3D cake-making game, at `/pasteleria`: solo levels, or a timed duel in a room), `vibora` (Viborita, at `/viborita`: slither-style snakes, in public online worlds or a friends' room) `triqui` (tic-tac-toe, also called ta-te-ti, with an "infinito" mode) `tronco` (El tronco, at `/el-tronco`: a 3D ring where a spinning log has to be jumped or kicked back; vs bots, online or friends), `puentes` (cross every bridge once: generated maps, solo levels/daily, race rooms and online) and `ladrillazo` (a brick breaker: solo campaign/infinite, race, attack, duel and co-op, in friends' rooms or online) and `minigolf` (generated holes; solo course, today's holes, friends' rooms and online, everyone on the same hole at once) and `empujones` (talos pushing each other off a shrinking island, on a big screen with the phones as controllers) and `golpe` (El gran golpe, at `/el-gran-golpe`: a 2D top-down heist, stealing diamonds past guards, cameras and lasers, everyone for themselves or red vs blue, with bots) and `gravedad` (Gravedad Loca, at `/gravedad-loca`: a 2D platformer where you send gravity to the ceiling or the walls; solo levels, races with friends and online, with bots). UI text is in neutral Spanish with tú ("Sube", "Elige") for every Spanish-speaking country: Talo is global, so no voseo and no regional slang ("computadora", not "compu"; "aquí", not "acá"). Regional flavor lives in content, spread across the countries that visit most (Argentina, México, Colombia, Perú, Chile…), e.g. En la misma onda's country decks.

Stack: Next.js 16 App Router + React 19, Tailwind v4, Konva/react-konva for the puzzle board, Firebase Realtime Database + Storage. **There is no backend of our own** — everything runs client-side, except one route handler that signs voice-chat tokens (`app/api/voz/token`). Firebase config comes from `NEXT_PUBLIC_FIREBASE_*` env vars (in `.env`, gitignored); when missing, pages render `FirebaseSetupNotice` instead of crashing. The Firebase project is `talo-play` (`.firebaserc`); rules live in `database.rules.json` / `storage.rules`. The Storage bucket needs the CORS config in `cors.json` (applied with `gcloud storage buckets update gs://talo-play.firebasestorage.app --cors-file=cors.json`, not by `firebase deploy`) because the puzzle loads its image with `crossOrigin` to cut it on a canvas.

## Architecture

### Shared by every game

- **Identity** (`hooks/useClientIdentity.ts`): random client id + player name in `localStorage` (`salon.*` keys, migrated from the old `rompecabezas.*`), shared across games. The name is asked only when about to play: lobbies via `PlayerIdentity`, rooms via `NamePrompt invitedTo=…` (tells invitees which game they're joining). The home never blocks on it.
- **Accounts and friends** (`lib/account.ts`, `components/AccountProvider.tsx`, `components/FriendInvite.tsx`, `app/amigos`): optional — playing never needs one. Firebase Auth with Google only (popup, redirect if blocked; in-app browsers like TikTok/Instagram get a "abrí en tu navegador" note). `AccountProvider` (root layout) makes the profile on the first sign-in from the device's client id and name, keeps `status/{uid}` online (`room`/`game` from `RoomHeader` via `setActivity`), and shows invites as a card with "Unirme". On another device the account's client id and name are adopted (`adoptIdentity`), keeping the device's own id in `salon.deviceId`; signing out gives the device its own id back (or a new one, `dropAccountIdentity`), so it's no longer the account's player — never while on a room path (`isRoomPath`), where the id is the player's seat. Data: `profiles/{uid}` (name, clientId, code), `codes/{code}` → uid (friend codes, 6 chars), `requests/{to}/{from}`, `friends/{a}/{b}` (both sides, written on accept), `blocks`, `status` (friends only), `invites/{to}` (friends only). Unlike `rooms/`, these paths are locked down in `database.rules.json` — deploy rules with `firebase deploy --only database`. `/amigos?agregar=CODE` prefills a friend code (the shared link). Rooms get "Amigos" in `RoomHeader` and a friends list in `InviteCard`; light pages get `FriendsLink` (with pending requests) in `SiteHeader`.
- **Community** (`app/ideas`, `lib/community.ts`, `components/IdeasButton.tsx`): `/ideas` lets people vote the next game (`CANDIDATES`, toggles at `community/votes/{candidate}/{clientId}`, public and informal) and send ideas/improvements/bugs to `feedback/{pushId}`, which is write-only (read it in the Firebase console). `SiteFooter` brings a floating "Ideas" button to every light page; the first time, after a while, it shows a bubble once (`talo.ideas.visto`). Rooms don't get it. Both paths have their own rules in `database.rules.json`.
- **La Plaza** (`lib/plaza.ts`, `components/PlazaProvider.tsx`, `PlazaDrawer.tsx`, `PlazaButton.tsx`, `PlazaBanner.tsx`, `PlazaCallButton.tsx`): Talo's global chat, to find people to play with, as a drawer over any page (from the right on wide screens, from the bottom on phones) — not a page: `PlazaProvider` (root layout) holds it, `usePlaza().show(where)` opens it (the floating "Plaza" button bottom left on light pages via `SiteFooter`, the home's `PlazaBanner`, a waiting room, or `?plaza=1`; `/plaza` redirects there). Tabs: Chat (rooms looking for players on top), Gente (who's in, rules) and Admin. The provider also counts everyone on Talo: each tab writes `online/{clientId}/{tab}` = server time, rewritten every 2 min while visible and removed on disconnect or after 5 min hidden; only players with a tab written in the last 5 min count (`ONLINE_STALE_MS`), since a forgotten background tab keeps its connection — people, not tabs, are counted, with or without an account; `useActiveCount` shows it on the button, the banner and the drawer. Quick phrases (`PLAZA_PHRASES`, stored by index: append only) work for anyone; free text needs a Google account, not banned. Waiting rooms have «Buscar gente en la Plaza» (`plaza/calls/{roomId}`, card with "Unirme", gone when the waiting room closes or after `CALL_TTL_MS`). The rules are the safety (no backend): every post writes `plaza/last/{clientId}` = now in the same update and must be 2.5 s after the previous one; text rejects links, mails, @ and 7+ digit numbers (the client checks the same with `hasContact` and covers insults with `softenText`); the "Admin · Talo" badge (`admin: true`) and deleting, banning (`plaza/bans/{uid}`) and pinning (`plaza/pinned`) are only accepted from accounts in `admins/` (set from the console or `firebase database:set /admins/{uid} true`). `HIDE_AT` reports (`plaza/reports`, anyone) hide a message until an admin restores it; muting is local (`talo.plaza.silenciados`). Messages over a day old and stale calls are deleted by whoever opens the Plaza (`cleanupPlaza`)..
- **Open rooms** (`lib/openRooms.ts`, `components/RoomAccess.tsx`, `components/OpenRoomsList.tsx`): `rooms/{id}/meta/access` is "privada" (default/absent: link, code, or while it asks the Plaza for people) or "abierta" (listed the whole time, also mid-game; latecomers enter like any link). Chosen in the waiting room (`RoomAccessCard`, "¿Quién puede entrar?") or mid-game from the lock/globe in `RoomHeader` (`RoomAccessButton`), by the host or anyone when there's no host or it's away. The listing `openRooms/{id}` (game, count, `playing`, host name) is rewritten every `BEAT_MS` by the room's connected player with the lowest id; the writer also sets it to go on disconnect, takes it down when leaving alone and re-lists it if it vanishes while it stays (`onUnlisted`); readers skip it after `TTL_MS` and delete very old ones; the rules accept it only while the meta says "abierta". `playing` = no `WaitingRoom` on screen (`markWaiting`). The 3D games' full-screen matches have no header, so their room clients render `OpenRoomBeacon`. Not offered where `RoomHeader` gets `social={false}` (public worlds, solo rooms). "Salas abiertas" (`OpenRoomsList`: open rooms + Plaza calls, `subscribeListedRooms`) shows on the home, each lobby (that game) and the Plaza's Chat tab.
- **Friend chats** (`lib/dm.ts`, `components/FriendChat.tsx`): `dms/{a}/{b}/{pushId}` with the uids sorted, readable by those two and writable only while they're friends; `dmSeen/{me}/{other}` marks it read. `useUnreadChats` feeds the dot on each friend in `/amigos` and the badge of `FriendsLink` (with friend requests).
- **Game catalog** (`lib/games.ts`, pure data — safe in server components): per game `title`, `tagline`, `min/maxPlayers`, `duration`, `contexts`, `howTo`; plus `CONTEXTS`, `GAME_ORDER`, `CATEGORIES` (the home's shelves: every game in exactly one — a new game needs one), `roomPath()`. It's the single source for home cards, lobbies, waiting rooms and link previews — add a game there first. Every room stores `rooms/{id}/meta/game` (older puzzle rooms have none = `rompecabezas`). Room codes are global: `/sala/[roomId]` looks the game up (`lib/roomLookup.ts`) and redirects; the home's "¿Te invitaron?" (`JoinRoomForm`) and old links use it. Each room client redirects to `/sala/{id}` if the meta is another game's.
- **New games** (`lib/newGames.ts`, `components/NewBadge.tsx`, `components/NewGameBubble.tsx`): a catalog `addedAt` (release day, UTC) makes a game "new" for `NEW_DAYS` (21) — set it when adding a game. While new it gets a yellow «Nuevo» `NewBadge` (home cards and rows, lobby band, similar games, landings) and a card in the home's "Juegos nuevos" row (newest four, hidden when none). Both are worked out in the browser after mount (`useNewGames`), so static pages never freeze the build day. `NewGameBubble` (in `SiteFooter`, over the Ideas button) tells people who played before (they have a name) about the newest one, once (`talo.nuevo.visto`); first visits are marked silently, its own lobby counts as seen, and the Ideas bubble waits while it is pending.
- **Host**: `meta.hostId` = the creator's client id (passed to each `create…Room`). Only the host sees "Empezar", unless there is no host (older rooms) or the host isn't connected — then anyone can (`startPermission` in `components/WaitingRoom.tsx`), so a room never gets stuck.
- **Inviting** (`lib/share.ts`, `components/InviteCard.tsx`): big code + WhatsApp (`wa.me`) + native share sheet + copy link, using the game's room URL. `RoomHeader` always has "Invitar". The big screens (Carreras, Arena, Fuego, El tronco, Empujones) show `RoomQR` (`components/RoomQR.tsx`: the room's phone link as a QR, drawn as SVG with `uqr`, no service) big in their join card and small in the corner during a match. Link previews: `app/opengraph-image.tsx` (brand image) and `generateMetadata` → `lib/inviteMetadata.ts` in every `[roomId]/page.tsx` ("¡Te invitaron a jugar X!"). `metadataBase` comes from `NEXT_PUBLIC_SITE_URL` (default https://taloplay.com, the site's domain).
- **SEO / GEO** (`lib/seo.ts`): every game folder has a `layout.tsx` with `gameMetadata(id)` + `gameJsonLd(id)` (schema.org `VideoGame`, `BreadcrumbList` and the game's `FAQPage` from `gameFaq`) — a new game needs one too. Lobbies (`GameLobby`) always render — also on the server, name or not — how to play, the full guide (`GuideBody light`, under "Reglas de…"), the FAQ and similar games; only the create form waits for a name, so crawlers read the whole page. Each game has its own link preview, `/og/{game}` (`app/og/[game]/route.tsx`: its `GameArt` scene, serialized with `lib/svgString.ts`, on its shelf's color), used by its lobby and by its rooms' invites. Landing pages for searches (`lib/landings.ts`: one per `PlayContext` and one per shelf, texts and game lists built from the catalog; each is a folder `app/{slug}/page.tsx` → `LandingPage`; a new shelf needs an entry) are linked from the footer's game directory (every light page lists every game), the home's shelves and each lobby. The home (`app/page.tsx`, server; the UI is `app/HomeClient.tsx`) adds `HOME_FAQ` (shown and sent as `FAQPage`). `app/sitemap.ts`, `app/robots.ts` and `/llms.txt` are built from the catalog. Rooms are `noindex` (`inviteMetadata`). Google Analytics (`@next/third-parties`, id `G-22BJ9CB5Y7`, overridable with `NEXT_PUBLIC_GA_ID`) loads only in production builds.
- **Modo TV** (`lib/tvMode.ts`, `components/TvModeButton.tsx`): a lighter profile for the big screens only (Carreras, Arena and Fuego in `Battle screen`, El tronco, Empujones), for smart TVs and TV boxes: drawn pixels capped at `TV_MAX_PIXELS` (1280×720, `tvDpr`; the TV scales it up), quality forced low (`useQuality(key, tv)`: no bloom, no real-time shadows; El tronco gets `lite`), no antialiasing, adaptive resolution down to `TV_MIN_SCALE`; Empujones' 2D canvas reads the budget every frame. On automatically on a TV browser (`isTvBrowser`: Tizen, webOS, Android/Google TV, Fire TV, Chromecast, VIDAA, Bravia…) and switchable with «Modo TV» on each screen (`talo.modoTv`: auto/si/no). A new big screen should take it.
- **3D quality** (`lib/postfx.ts`, `hooks/useQuality.ts`, `components/QualityButton.tsx`): the 3D games (Fuego cruzado, Arena medieval, Carreras 3D) take `quality` "alta" (bloom via `Bloom` + real-time shadows) or "baja"; it's remembered per game (`fuego.calidad`, `arena.calidad`, `carreras.calidad`), defaulting to high with a mouse and low on touch. Bloom threshold is ~1, so only colors pushed over 1 on purpose (`glowColor`: flames, lights, brake lights, visors) glow — keep plain whites at or below 1. Lights used by effects are created up front (no shader recompiles mid-game). Carreras only blooms a single full-canvas view (split screens draw plain).
- **Brand** (from a design handoff kept out of the repo, `talo-handoff/` locally; code comments citing it refer to that): Talo's own pages (home, lobbies, the name prompt) are light (`.talo-light`, white page, yellow `#FFD400` hero, flat category colors); rooms and games stay dark on the ink page (`#111111`). Tokens are Tailwind colors in `app/globals.css` (`bg-talo-hero`, `text-talo-ink`, `bg-talo-surface`…). Fonts: Albert Sans (UI), Dela Gothic One (`font-display`: titles, logo, big numbers; one weight, so `font-synthesis-weight: none`), JetBrains Mono (`font-code`, room-code boxes), Geist Mono (`font-mono`, in-game numbers). No gradients, glows, glassmorphism or tilted elements. Each shelf has a flat color (`CATEGORY_COLORS`, `gameColors(game)` in `lib/talos.ts`), used by lobby bands, the waiting room's table and `GameIcon`.
- **Talos** (`components/Talo.tsx`, `lib/talos.ts`, `lib/taloEvents.ts`): the brand's characters, flat shapes with eyes and expressions. A player's talo is `playerTalo(id, color, size)`: shape from the client id, body in the player's color, the same on every screen. They are a read-only layer: game engines never import them, and they only react to public events through `reduceTalos` (`player_joined` in `TaloTable`, the waiting room's table; `game_ended`/`round_ended` in `GameResults`). Games with hidden info (`HIDDEN_INFO_GAMES`) only allow joins/turns mid-match (`eventAllowed`). Never feed them roles, hands, secret words, hidden positions, unrevealed scores or decision times.
- **Icons**: presentation UI (headers, waiting room, rules) uses `lucide-react` icons, not emojis — game icons live in `components/GameIcon.tsx` (`GameIcon`, a flat tile in the game's shelf color; `CONTEXT_ICONS`). Emojis inside the games are game content and stay.
- **Instructions** (`lib/guides.ts`, `components/HowToPlay.tsx`, `components/GuideArt.tsx`): every game has a full guide (goal, numbered sections, tips) besides the catalog's three-step `howTo`. Sections may name an `art` — a small inline SVG diagram drawn by `GuideArt`. The guide shows in the lobby ("Ver las reglas completas"), in the waiting room ("Cómo se juega") and from the "Reglas" button every `RoomHeader` has, also mid-game (`RulesDialog`). A new game needs a guide.
- **Room settings** (`components/RoomSettings.tsx`, `lib/roomSettings.ts`): a game's settings are declared once as a `GameSettings<T>` (in `app/{game}/settings.tsx`, or `components/{game}/settings.tsx` for the 3D games and carreras): fields (`choice`, `multi`, `toggle`, `custom`, `note`), `problem`, `create`, `save`, `fromMeta`. The lobby renders it with `CreateRoomForm`; the waiting room gets `RoomSettingsEditor` through `WaitingRoom`'s `settings` prop, shown to whoever may start (`startPermission`). `saveRoomSettings` writes the meta (and any fields a game copies into its state, e.g. Parchís `rules`, Truco/Dominó `target`, Backgammon `matchTo`, Oca `dice`, Fuego `map`/`mode`) only while the state is `waiting` (Verdad o reto: anytime). Room clients follow the meta live with `watchRoomMeta` (not a one-time `get`), so everyone sees the change. After a game, `BackToRoomButton` (`backToWaiting`: status `waiting`, `gameNo` + 1, a new `seed`) brings the room back to change settings; start functions must therefore build a fresh game from any leftover state. Rompecabezas has no waiting room, so its settings stay fixed. Games without settings keep their simple create form.
- **UI pieces**: `TaloLogo`/`TaloMark` ("tal" + a talo as the "o"; `tone` ink on light pages, hero yellow on dark), `SiteHeader`/`SiteFooter` (nav inside each light page's colored hero; black footer with the giant wordmark), `HeroCrowd` (the home's talos, pupils follow the cursor unless reduced motion), `GameArt` (each game's illustration: talos doing that game, flat SVG on a 120×120 canvas with no background, shown on the shelf color as the catalog thumbnail, the featured cover and the lobby band — a new game needs a scene), `JoinRoomForm` (code typed over six boxes; codes have letters and digits; «Escanear QR», `components/QrScanner.tsx`, reads a room QR with the back camera — the browser's `BarcodeDetector`, else `jsqr` loaded on open — and `lib/qrTarget.ts` sends a Talo link to its path or a bare code to `/sala/{code}`), `GameLobby` (band in the shelf's color, name, the game's create form as children on an ink panel since it shares the rooms' dark controls, how to play), `WaitingRoom` (pre-game screen: the table of talos with the host tagged, settings summary, `InviteCard` with the code in boxes, how to play, start), `RoomHeader`, `RoomStatus`, `DieFace` (a die with the roll animation), `GameResults` (`components/Results.tsx`: the end screen for scored games — podium, ranking with shared places, `rankFrom(players, scores)`; board games keep their own banner over the final board and add `Confetti` when you win), `components/ui.ts` (shared Tailwind class strings). One-on-one board games (Otelo, Backgammon) share their seat logic: `lib/twoSeats.ts` (pure join/take/leave/swap over `seats.a/b`, run inside each game's transaction) and `components/TwoSeatPicker.tsx` (seats in the waiting room, `Spectators`, `seatName`).
- **Full screen and zoom** (`lib/fullscreen.ts`, `components/FullscreenButton.tsx`, `app/manifest.ts`, `components/ZoomGuard.tsx`): every game's full-screen button is `FullscreenButton` (Fullscreen API with the `webkit` fallback on Android, iPad and computers; on iPhone, which has none for pages, it explains "Agregar a inicio"; hidden when already running from the home screen). The manifest (+ `appleWebApp`) makes Talo open as an app from the home screen. Page zoom stays off for the games: the viewport says `user-scalable=no` (Android), `ZoomGuard` cancels iOS's pinch gesture and `touch-action: manipulation` on `html` drops double-tap zoom; games that zoom themselves (the puzzle) still get their touches.
- **Controller mode** (`components/ControllerShell.tsx`): every phone used as a big-screen game's controller (Carreras' driver, Fuego cruzado's and Arena's `Pad`, Empujones' match, El tronco's control) fills the whole screen — `ControllerShell`, a fixed layer with no room header and no scroll, safe-area padding — and puts `ControllerActions` (full screen + «Salir», which asks and goes back to the game's lobby) in its top row. A new controller must do the same.
- **Adjustable phone controllers** (`lib/padLayout.ts`, `components/PadKit.tsx`): the big-screen games' phone pads (Carreras' `Controller` — also over the race in telefono mode, with its own layout —, Arena's and Empujones' `Pad`, El tronco's controller buttons) place each control as a fraction of the pad's area with a size multiplier (`PadControl`, `padDefaults`, `padBox`). `PadSurface` lays them out, with an optional stick (under the thumb on the left part, or fixed) and a drag-to-look zone; «Ajustar» (`AdjustButton`) opens its editor: drag, size per control, transparency, the game's sensitivity (Arena: looking; Empujones: the stick; Carreras: the tilt) and a fixed stick, saved per device (`arena.controles`, `empujones.controles`, `carreras.controles2.pantalla`/`.horizontal`/`.celular`: upright, sideways and over the race, `tronco.controles`). A new big-screen pad should use it. Fuego cruzado keeps its own richer editor (`components/fuego/touchLayout.ts`).
- **Ranking** (`lib/rank.ts`, `hooks/useAwardMatch.ts`, `app/ranking`, `components/RankingTeaser.tsx`): every room game calls `useAwardMatch(game, roomId, matchKey, outcome)` (before any early return) when a match ends, with my `place` (1 = won, ties shared via `placeIn`, null = no winner), `players` (teams count as 2) and `vsBotsOnly` only for visible bots — «Jugar en línea»'s hidden bots, public worlds and Viborita's bots count as people. Points: `PLAY` + `PLACE_BONUS`, half vs visible bots, `DAILY_CAP` a day; once per match key per browser; pending points flush to `rank/all/{clientId}` and `rank/weeks/w{monday}/{clientId}` within the rules' bounds (`MAX_WRITE` per write, `GAP_MS` apart, never down; an entry with a `uid` — the account's own client id — can only be touched by it). The public tables (/ranking: week and all-time, the home's top 5) show only entries with a `uid`. Solo modes and big screens never award; Verdad o reto never ends. A new game must call `useAwardMatch`.
- **Installable app and push** (`app/manifest.ts`, `public/sw.js`, `components/ServiceWorker.tsx`, `lib/install.ts`, `components/InstallButton.tsx`, `lib/push.ts`, `components/PushCard.tsx`, `app/api/push/route.ts`): the root layout registers the service worker (push + notification clicks; deliberately no caching, so every visit gets the latest deploy) and catches the browser's install prompt early. «Instalar Talo» (`InstallButton`, the home's card after the open rooms, and the notifications card on iPhone) opens that prompt, or on iPhone/iPad explains Compartir → Agregar a inicio; it hides when running installed. Push is standard Web Push with our VAPID keys (`NEXT_PUBLIC_VAPID_PUBLIC_KEY`, `VAPID_PRIVATE_KEY`, `VAPID_SUBJECT`; without them the card hides and the route answers 503), for accounts only: «Notificaciones» on /amigos saves the device's subscription at `push/{uid}/{key}`. `notify(kind, to)` runs after sending an invite, a friend request, accepting one and a DM; `/api/push` reads everything through the RTDB REST API with the sender's own ID token, so the rules decide (sender is a friend, or has a pending request to them, before `push/{to}` can be read) — no admin credentials. The service worker skips showing it when a Talo tab is focused. iPhone only gets push from the installed app.
- **Turn alert** (`hooks/useTurnAlert.ts`): turn-based games call `useTurnAlert(myTurn)` (before any early return): a short vibration when the turn arrives and a blinking "¡Te toca!" tab title while the page is hidden.
- **Sounds** (`lib/sfx.ts`, `hooks/useSound.ts`): short synthesized effects for the 2D games (`playSound`: place, capture, turn, good, bad, dice, tick, win, end, flip), muted from the header (`talo.sonido`, shared); the audio unlocks on the first tap. `useSoundOn(key, name)` plays when a move counter changes (not on arriving). `useTurnAlert` chimes and `Confetti`/`GameResults` play win/end on their own. The 3D games and Pastelería keep their own audio.
- **Voice chat** (`lib/voice.ts`, `components/VoiceButton.tsx`, `app/api/voz/token/route.ts`): LiveKit Cloud (SFU). `RoomHeader` shows "Voz" (unless `social={false}`: Viborita's public worlds) when `NEXT_PUBLIC_LIVEKIT_URL` is set; the route — our only server code — signs the token with `LIVEKIT_API_KEY`/`LIVEKIT_API_SECRET` after checking via RTDB REST that the caller is in the room's presence and it isn't `meta.online`. LiveKit room = `talo-{roomId}`, identity = client id. The connection is a module singleton (survives header remounts and fullscreen 3D games); `VoiceKeeper` in the root layout leaves when the path no longer has the room code. Silencing someone is local (`setVolume(0)`).
- **Text chat** (`lib/chat.ts`, `components/ChatButton.tsx`): "Chat" in every `RoomHeader` (not with `social={false}`), plain pushes to `rooms/{id}/chat` (last 60 read, 200 chars, a short gap between sends), with an unread counter and a bubble for new messages. Games where typing gives the answer away pass `chatLocked` (a reason) during those phases: Dibujo `drawing`, Emojis `question`, Impostor `clues`/`voting`/`guess`, Mímica `acting`, Palabra `racing`, Lobo at night and for whoever sees every role (dead, spectators) mid-game. A new guessing game should do the same.
- **Most played** (`lib/playStats.ts`): `RoomHeader` (in every room) calls `countPlay`, which adds 1 to `rooms/_stats/plays/{game}` by transaction once per browser and room (localStorage `talo.jugada.{roomId}`). The home's "Los que más se juegan" is `mostPlayed(stats, 4, CURATED)`: the curated four until there are `MIN_PLAYS` in total. It sits under `rooms/` because that's what the rules open; keys starting with "_" are never rooms, and the cleanup skips them.
- **Tracking** (`lib/analytics.ts`, `components/PageViews.tsx`, `lib/dailyStats.ts`, `app/estadisticas`): GA events via `trackEvent` (typed list in `lib/analytics.ts`; production only). Page views on client navigation are sent by `PageViews` (root layout) once the new title is in, because GA's own history page views read the old title — GA's enhanced measurement "browser history events" must stay off. `PageViews` also `set`s `page_title` (so "¡Te toca!" never shows as a page) and counts the day's visit. Our own per-day counts live at `rooms/_stats/days/d{yyyymmdd}` (the UTC day: `visits`, `new`, `plays/{game}` from `countPlay`, `rooms/{game}` from `create_room`), production only, bots skipped; the internal, unlinked, noindex `/estadisticas` page reads them and can mark a browser as the team's (`talo.noContar`: not counted, GA `traffic_type: internal`). `app/not-found.tsx` sends `page_not_found` with the path.
- **Presence** (`lib/presence.ts`, `hooks/useRoomPresence.ts`): connected players with name/color, removed via `onDisconnect`. An account shares one client id on every device, so two devices of one player share an entry: when one leaves and removes it, the other writes it back.
- **Old rooms cleanup** (`lib/roomCleanup.ts`): there's no backend, so visitors' browsers do it. `rooms/_index/{roomId}` = last activity (ms), touched by `joinPresence` on entering, every 30 min while connected and on disconnect. The home and every lobby call `cleanupOldRooms` (at most every 30 min per browser): it deletes up to 25 rooms idle for over `MAX_IDLE_DAYS` (3) with empty presence, and indexes up to 25 rooms from before the index (REST `shallow` listing + `meta/createdAt`). `_index` can't clash with a room code. Storage files are deleted by the bucket's lifecycle rule (`storage-lifecycle.json`, 10 days, applied with `gcloud storage buckets update gs://talo-play.firebasestorage.app --lifecycle-file=storage-lifecycle.json`), not by the app.
- **Seeded randomness** (`lib/random.ts`): `mulberry32`, `seededShuffle`. Anything derived from a seed stored in the room must stay deterministic across clients; changing it alters existing rooms.
- **No host**: shared game state (turns, rounds, races) is only mutated through `runTransaction` (`lib/transact.ts` wraps it with a normalizer), so concurrent clients can't double-apply a move. Timers/delays are scheduled by every client and made idempotent by the transaction (see `resolveMismatch`), so one player leaving can't freeze a game.

### Rompecabezas deslizante (`app/deslizante`, `lib/deslizante.ts`)

### 4 en línea (`app/4-en-linea`, `lib/cuatro.ts`)

- `rooms/{id}/cuatro/state` — `seats` (`a` red / `b` yellow → client id), `cells` (a 42-char string, row-major from the top: `.` empty, `a`/`b` a disc), `turn`, `starter` (alternates on each "Revancha"), `lastMove`, `winner` (`a`/`b`/`draw`) and `wins` per client id. Everyone else in the room watches.

- «Jugar en línea» (`lib/matchmaking.ts`, `components/OnlineTabs.tsx`, `lib/boardBot.ts`, `hooks/useBotTurn.ts`), shared by the one-on-one games (Triqui, 4 en línea, Otelo, Damas, Ajedrez, Batalla naval, Backgammon, Dominó): the lobby's «En línea» tab looks for a rival in `rooms/_match/{queue}` (one transaction claims someone waiting under `FRESH_MS`, or queues me; the claimer creates the room with both seated — `onlineSeats`, colors at random — and writes its code in the waiter's entry). If nobody claims me in 3–7 s, I get a bot (`makeBotOpponent`): **on purpose it passes for a person** — a name from `RIVAL_NAMES`, a normal client id (`state.botId`), a random level (`randomBotLevel`), present in the room (`useBotPresence`: my client writes its presence) and a human-like pause (`botDelay`, sometimes instant, sometimes a long think; scaled per game). Never show anything that gives it away. Every client in the room plays the bot's turn through the game's own move function; the move-count guard lets one through. Online rooms (`state.online`) skip the waiting room and have `social={false}` (no chat a bot couldn't answer). Brains: `lib/triquiBot.ts`, `lib/cuatroBot.ts` (negamax + alpha-beta), `lib/oteloBot.ts` (square weights + mobility, last 10 empties exact on difícil), `lib/damasBot.ts` (minimax on the game's `legalMoves`/`applyMove`, a capture chain is one step per call), `lib/ajedrezBot.ts` (root moves from the full rules, then its own numeric board: alpha-beta, quiescence, piece-square tables; no castling/en passant below the root); searches are time-boxed by `deepen`. `lib/navalBot.ts` places a random fleet (taking a person's time) and only uses what a player sees (hits, misses, sunk ships; difícil fires by a placement-count density). `lib/backgammonBot.ts` lists every legal way to play the roll (use-all-dice and larger-die rules), scores the end positions and plays the best one a step per call (noise seeded by turn + end position, so the plan holds mid-turn); it never doubles and takes a double unless well behind. `lib/dominoBot.ts` (one on one, seats a/c): heavy tiles and doubles first, on difícil keeping ends it holds. In Backgammon and Dominó the bot also presses «Siguiente partida/mano». Online Backgammon is to 3 points and online Dominó one on one to 100. In Damas and Ajedrez the bot accepts a draw offer when it isn't ahead. Rooms from the first version have the bot as id "bot" (`LEGACY_BOT_ID`).

### ¿Quién soy? (`app/quien-soy`, `lib/quiensoy.ts`, `lib/quiensoyCards.ts`)

- Everyone gets a character; the table shows everyone else's on a post-it over their talo and yours as «?». `meta`: `askMode` (`voz`: asked out loud, the asker taps «Ya pregunté»; `app`: typed), `source` (`mazo`: `dealFromDecks(decks, seed, n)`, distinct cards from `QUIEN_DECKS` — "Name|alt|alt", other accepted names; `postit`: a `writing` phase where each writes the next one's, `targetOf`/`writerOf`, `submitPostit`; `fillPostits` deals the missing ones from the decks when a writer left or after `WRITE_GRACE_MS`). Cards are copied into `state.cards` (readable with DevTools — accepted).
- `quiensoy/state`: `order` (fixed per round), `turn` + `turnNo` (guard) + `turnAsked`. The asker `ask`s; the others (connected, in the round, also those already done) `answer` Sí/No/A veces/No sé; it resolves with all of them or on `ANSWER_SECONDS` (`closeQuestion`, also the asker's «Cerrar»), logs the tally (`log`), and the turn passes on more no than yes or after `MAX_PER_TURN`. On your turn you can `guess` (checked locally with `guessMatches`: alt names, no articles/accents, small typos, a famous person's surname) — right: `pointsFor(asked, misses)` = `GUESS_BASE` − questions − 2 × misses (min `GUESS_MIN`), done; wrong: a miss and the turn passes — or `passTurn`; `giveUp` anytime. `skipAbsent` skips a turn holder who left (ends the round if everyone still guessing left). ### Damas (`app/damas`, `lib/damas.ts`)

- Seats like 4 en línea; `a` = blancas (bottom, move first), `b` = negras, whose board is drawn flipped. `cells` is a 64-char string (`.`, `a`/`b` men, `A`/`B` kings). No legal moves = loss.
- `makeMove` is one step/jump guarded by `moveNo`. Also `resign` and `offerDraw` (the rival offering too = draw; any move clears the offer). "Revancha" swaps seats so colors alternate.

### Ahorcado (`app/ahorcado`, `lib/ahorcado.ts`)

### Sopa de letras (`app/sopa-de-letras`, `lib/sopa.ts`)

- The board is `buildSoup(seed, theme, level)` — fully deterministic (words from `lib/sopaWords.ts`, append-only; directions per `LEVELS`). Changing the generator or the lists changes existing rooms' boards.
- Players mark a word by tapping its first and last letter (`lineBetween` + `wordAt`, either direction). `claimWord` writes `found/{WORD}` = clientId if nobody did; the last word ends it (most words wins, `wins` counted). Countdown like the races (`startedAt`).

### Carreras and Carreras 3D (`app/carreras`, `app/carreras-3d`, `components/carreras`, `lib/carreras*.ts`)

- Two catalog games on the same code: route pages just pass `game` to the shared `Lobby`, `RoomClient` and `ScreenClient`. `carreras` is always `pantalla` mode, drawn top-down in 2D. `carreras3d` draws in 3D and can also run in `telefono` mode.
- Up to 4 seats (`a`–`d`, like 4 en línea's). `meta.mode` picks where the race runs. In both modes `carreras/state` changes only by transaction. It holds `racers` (seats at the start), the `startedAt` countdown, `results` (ms per seat, `reportFinish` once each) and `firstAt`. The race closes when every racer finished, or `FINISH_GRACE_MS` after the first finish, or early if the rest left (`closeRace`, any client via `useRaceCloser`). `winner` = fastest.
- `pantalla`: a screen at `/carreras/{id}/pantalla` (TV/projector; the lobby sends the creator there) is the authority. It simulates every car from `carreras/input/{clientId}` (`{s, b}`, throttled by `useInputSender`, removed `onDisconnect`; no input = coast), reports each finish and writes `carreras/live` (lap/place) for the phones. It never joins presence, so phones see the host as away and anyone can start. View: top-down 2D (`lib/carrerasDraw.ts`: hills shade and cast shadows, bridges and the cars on them are drawn over the rest, flying cars grow) for `carreras`, split-screen 3D (1, 2 or 2×2) for `carreras3d`. A/D/S and the arrows drive seats a/b for testing.
- `telefono`: no screen. Each phone runs `PhoneRace`: it simulates its own car, publishes `carreras/cars/{clientId}` (`CarPose`, ~15 Hz, tagged with `gameNo`), and draws rivals extrapolated from their pose and eased. Collisions with rivals only push your own car (`collideWithGhost`). Each phone reports its own finish.
- `lib/carrerasTrack.ts` is pure: tracks are Catmull-Rom centerlines in their own world (`def.size`, 2000–2600 wide) with walls at half the width. `progress` (arc length driven) gives laps and places. Gas is automatic (`gas: false` coasts after the finish). `stepCar`/`collideCars`/`collideWithGhost` return the hit speed (for sound and sparks). Each track has a `theme` (campo, atardecer, desierto, nieve, noche) that only changes the looks.
- Mario Kart-ish features: control points may carry a height (`hs` per sample; hills slow you down going up), `ramps` and `boosts` are `TrackFeature`s placed by control-point index (+ fraction) and a side/span across the road, resolved to `Zone`s. `groundAt` = hills + ramp wedge. Cars have `z`/`vz`/`air`/`boost`: on the road they follow it unless it drops away faster than gravity (ramp lips, sharp crests), then fly ballistically with little steering; boost pads kick the speed up to `BOOST_TOP`. Parts of a track may cross when one is `BRIDGE_CLEARANCE` higher (`trackProblem` checks that, slopes, a flat start and the world bounds); cars at different heights don't collide. Track shapes were tuned by rendering them — keep `trackProblem` null for every fixed track.
- `aleatoria` track: `getTrack("aleatoria", seed)` generates it from `carreras/state.trackSeed` (set at creation; `newTrack` while waiting, `rematch(…, true)` = "Revancha en otra pista"). The generator places points around the center in angle order and keeps the longest of the first few that pass `trackProblem` (overlap, too tight, outside, short); same seed = same track on every client. Use `trackSeedOf(meta, state)`: it's 0 for fixed tracks so they never rebuild.
- Tunnels (`TrackDef.tunnels`, [from, to] control points → `track.tunnels` zones, `inTunnel`): 3D draws concrete walls, an arched roof at `TUNNEL_HEIGHT`, striped portals and roof lights (`buildTunnels`); corner signs, billboards and lamps stay out; under one the race audio echoes (`RaceAudio.setTunnel`). `trackProblem` refuses a ramp inside or another part of the track crossing one. Gran Premio, Cañón, Nocturna and Centro have them.
- Long tracks (`long: true`: Gran Premio, Cañón, Nocturna, Centro — a ~20k sunset city with two tunnels —, ~14–20k long in 4200–5200 wide worlds) are 3D only (`tracksFor(is3d)`; `normalizeMeta` sends a 2D room back to the óvalo): from above their cars would be tiny. `city` (Nocturna) swaps most trees for lit buildings. Designing one is iterating points until `trackProblem` is null; `lapSeconds` estimates a lap.
- Drifts, tricks and slipstream (`lib/carrerasTrack.ts`, no new buttons: `CarInput.drift`, the «Derrape» button — Shift on a keyboard, `d` in the pad wire; bots never press it): holding it while steering past `DRIFT_STEER` (either order) above `DRIFT_FROM` drifts (less grip, turns into the drift, charges `DRIFT_TIERS` blue/orange/purple, released for a mini-turbo via `burst`; a wall crash or flying cancels it); a tap of it in the air spins a trick (`TRICK_SECONDS`), landed after it ends for a boost, still spinning costs speed; `slipstream(car, others, dt)`, called by each runner after stepping, charges behind a car at speed for a push. Cars count `pads`/`minis`/`purples`/`tricks`/`drafts` (missions read them via `RaceStatsTracker`). The scene shows them from the pose (`stuntOf`: `drift` signed 1 + tier → colored sparks at the rear wheels, `spin` → a barrel roll); phones send them as `d`/`k` in `CarPose` only when present. Bots' lap times didn't change (checked headless).
- Power-ups (3D only; `lib/carrerasItems.ts`, pure): rows of boxes at three spots per lap (`itemBoxes`); a box gives a power after `ROLL_SECONDS` (`takeBoxes`, `rollItem` by place: the leader gets defense, the back turbos and rockets): turbo, triple turbo, shield (`car.shield`, takes one hit), oil (`Slick`, dropped behind) and a rocket (`Rocket`, chases the car ahead along the track by progress). Hits call `spinOutCar` (`car.spinOut`: no control, gas on, `SPIN_OUT_DRAG`). «Poder» is a press counter (`CarInputWire.i`, E on a keyboard, E or «/» for the screen's test seats). Pantalla: the screen runs everything in `ItemsRun` and sends each phone its power in `LiveCar.it`/`ro`. Telefono: each phone runs the cars it runs (its own, the bots if host) and shares oil and rockets at `carreras/items/g{n}` (`dropSlick`, `fireRocket`, `subscribeItems`, server-stamped); whoever runs the target decides the hit; poses carry `sh`/`o` for the shield and spin-out. Bots use theirs with `botUsesItem`. The scene draws them with `setItemWorld`.
- Bots: `meta.bots` (0–3) and `meta.botLevel` (fácil/normal/difícil) are copied into the state as `botCount`/`botLevel` (the start's transaction only sees the state). `raceFrom` fills that many free seats into `state.bots`; their `seats` entry stays "" so people can still sit while waiting (`isBotSeat` only counts during a race; `driverName` → "Rayo (bot)"). `botInput` (pure, `lib/carrerasTrack.ts`) aims at a point ahead on its own lane, pulled to the inside of the next bend and onto boost pads it likes, and brakes for the tightest bend within braking distance; levels scale corner margin, top speed, steering wobble and boost use; `pace` is a light rubber band against the people's leader. Pantalla: the screen drives them. Telefono: the bot host (lowest connected id) simulates them as real `Car`s and publishes `cars/bot-{seat}` (`sendBotPose`, no `onDisconnect`: the next host rebuilds them with `carFromPose`) and reports their finishes. `useRaceCloser` treats bots as present.
- Lap times: whoever runs a car keeps a `LapClock` and sends the best lap with `reportFinish(…, bestMs)` → `state.best`; `Standings` shows each best lap and marks the fastest. HUDs show the lap's time when it ends, "¡Última vuelta!" and "¡Contramano!" (`wrongWay`); the phone shows speed and the running lap clock, written straight into the DOM. In development `window.__carrerasAuto = true` drives your own phone car with the bot brain (for testing).
- `lib/carreras3d.ts` (three.js, browser only) builds the scene from the sampled centerline, dressed by the theme (gradient sky, clouds, stars/lamps at night, snowfall, cacti…, grandstand by the start, start lights via `setStartLights`). 2D (x, y) maps to (x, height, y) and `rotation.y = -heading`. Raised road sits on earth banks, or on a deck with pillars where it passes over another part (`bridgeSamples`); ramps are striped wedges and boost pads scroll their arrows. Wheel spin, steering, pitch, brake lights, tire smoke, landing dust, boost flames and sparks are derived from the poses alone (`setPose` with velocity, `z` and `ground`), so rivals get them too (phones send `z` and lift `l` in `CarPose`); `impact` adds a spark burst and camera shake. Cameras: behind (`detras`) or cockpit (`cabina`).
- Cars (`carGeometry`, built once per scene and shared, `userData.shared` skips disposal per car): a GT body extruded from its profile, fenders, glass cabin under a painted roof, splitter, skirts, diffuser, exhausts, wing, twin stripes in the seat's `accent` and the race number on the doors (`CarLook`: color, accent, number, label); merged per material. Track dressing: chevron boards and tire stacks on sharp bends (`buildCorners`, from `curvatures`), Talo ad boards along straights (`buildBillboards`), a second grandstand on long tracks, buildings (`buildCity`). Shadows: the shadow map is drawn at least once from the first frame (`shadowReady`; the big screen starts on an aerial view with no car followed, and materials first drawn without a shadow map make WebGL skip everything lit afterwards). Performance: adaptive resolution (`adapt` lowers the pixel ratio down to 60% when frames are slow), one shadow map per frame around every followed car (`shadowMap.autoUpdate = false`), runoff/curbs/lines/walls merged into one mesh, static objects' matrices frozen after `build` (mark anything that moves with `userData.moving`), name tags hidden when a car is right in front of the camera.
- Progress (Carreras 3D only; `lib/carrerasProgress.ts`, `lib/carrerasProgressSync.ts`, like Arena's): XP and levels (Arena's curve), three daily missions (`MISSIONS`, `dailyMissions`), looks unlocked by level — paint (`asiento` keeps the seat's color), stripes, rims, boost flames, neon, title, race number — never a driving advantage. Kept in `carreras.progreso`, on the account at `carrerasProgress/{uid}` (owner-only rules). The lobby's `CarCard` (with `CarPreview3D`, `previewCar`) shows level, missions and «Personalizar». `RoomClient` sends `look`/`lv` with the player; `seatCarLook` (shared.tsx) turns a seat into the 3D `CarLook` (bots get `botCarLook`), and the tag keeps the seat's color. Whoever runs a car (the big screen, or the phone; `RaceStatsTracker`: boost pads, jumps, air time, wall hits, passes, laps) writes `carreras/stats/{seat}` tagged with the race number when it finishes or the race closes; the driver's phone (`useRaceReward`) applies it once per race (`carreras.ultimaCarrera`) and shows `RaceRewardCard` under the standings.
- Modeled cars (`lib/carreras3dModels.ts`, `public/models/carreras/`, made in Meshy, meshopt-compressed; originals in the Meshy project): six bodies (`CAR_BODIES`, the «Carrocería» cosmetic `BODIES`) without wheels and two wheels. A body is in meters, nose +z, on y = 0; `carMesh` turns it a quarter and scales it by `MODEL_SCALE` (8.5: 4 m = the 34-unit car), swaps its materials by name (Pintura, Franjas, Vidrio, Negro, Cromo, Faros, Luces traseras; wheels Neumático, Llanta) for the car's own, puts the wheels at the centers measured on each model's wells (`x`, `front`, `back`, `r`; they roll by `wheelR`) and the number on both doors. Same hitbox for every body. Until the models load the drawn car shows, and `setCars` rebuilds it after.
- Track dressing (`public/models/carreras/deco/deco-*.glb`, Meshy, meshopt; `loadDecoModels`, `decoInstances` in lib/carreras3dModels.ts): once loaded, `RaceScene.decorate` swaps the drawn ramps and boost pads (stretched to their zone), tire stacks, trees/cacti/snowy pines (by theme) and city blocks (three building kinds) for instanced models at the same spots (`this.deco` records them while building), and adds a portal at each tunnel mouth. It also swaps the start gantry (the working start bulbs sit on its light discs), billboards (the drawn ad faces go on its panel), lamps (city tracks get them by day too, unlit) and grandstands (a cheering crowd on its seat rows), and adds fences along straights and cones by ramps and box rows. Quantized files are baked to float positions first (else they clamp to ±1) and zero normals are fixed (one NaN pixel and bloom blacks out the whole screen). Pieces with more than 24 copies (trees, fences, buildings) are split into 700-unit zones of the map (`ZONE_SIZE`, `userData.zone`), so frustum culling works per zone and `render` hides the zones past the fog in each view (before, every tree was drawn in every view: 2.6 M triangles a view on Gran Premio). In Modo TV the drawn trees stay (a few dozen triangles each instead of ~3000) and there are no fences. Livelier than the files: colors a touch more saturated, metal/glass/painted parts shiny, per-instance tints (leaves, facades, night windows), leaves sway (`breeze`), boost arrows pulse. «Luz» parts glow softly at night only. Objects the scene built have frozen matrices: after moving one in `decorate`, call `updateMatrix()`.
- Recorded sounds (`public/sounds/carreras/*.mp3`, 25, made with ElevenLabs sound effects on the Creator plan — commercial license; regenerate with the same prompts, key in `.env.local` as `ELEVENLABS_API_KEY`, never committed): `RaceAudio.loadSamples` decodes them; one-shots play the recording (`sample`) and fall back to the synthesized sound if it didn't load; with all the loops in, each car's voice runs `motor-ralenti` when stopped, then one steady tone (`motor-parejo`) pitched by speed (mostly speed, a little gearbox so it never jumps; lower and quieter off the gas), `arranque` pulling away and a quiet `cambio` on upshifts.
- Sound (`lib/carrerasAudio.ts`): everything synthesized with Web Audio — engines with gear shifts, tire screech, hits, jumps/landings, boosts, countdown beeps, lap chimes, finish fanfare. Stunts too, told apart by the car's own counts (`RaceCues.stunt`): drift crackle and a chime per spark color, a mini-turbo blast by color, the trick's spin and its landing («ta-dá» or a thud), the slipstream's wind (a voice per car) and push. `RaceCues` turns per-frame facts into one-off sounds. The context resumes on the first tap/key, so `RaceAudio` lives for the whole page, not per track. Muted state in `localStorage` (`carreras.sound`). The screen plays every car (panned per split view); in telefono mode each phone plays its own car, plus nearby rivals more quietly.
- Throttle: «Acelerador» Automático (default) or Manual (`carreras.acelerador`; the «Acelerar» button, ↑ or W), sent as `m`/`g` in the input wire; `gasOf(wire)` gives the car its gas (off = coasting); holding «Derrape» also accelerates in Manual (no thumb is left for «Acelerar»).
- Controls (`components/carreras/Controller.tsx`): while driving in pantalla mode the pad takes the whole phone screen (`Driver`: a fixed layer, one top row with the car, lap/place, mode, throttle, «Ajustar» and «Salir» back to the lobby); buttons, or tilt (gravity projected on the screen's x axis from `deviceorientation`; iOS needs `requestPermission` from a tap and HTTPS). It works as a page panel (pantalla) or as an overlay on the 3D view (telefono). On computers the keys drive too (arrows or A/D steer, down arrow, S or space brake; held keys win over tilt); with a fine pointer the overlay shows only a key hint instead of the big touch zones ("Usar botones" brings them back) and the panel adds the hint.

### Arena medieval (`app/arena-medieval`, `components/arena`, `lib/arena*.ts`)

- State and rules are pure in `lib/arenaRules.ts` (reducers `(state, …, now) → state | null`; `lib/arena.ts` runs them in transactions and re-exports them), so a headless sim can run the real rules. Modes (`meta.mode`, copied into `state.mode`; absent = `equipos`): `equipos` (below); `todos` (FFA, `meta.ffa` 3–8 knights; `roster` still alternates red/blue for older clients, sides come from `sideOf` = own id; `fscore` rounds won, `champ`/`champion`, `rk` round KOs for the timeout; each knight its own color, `fighterColor`); `rey` (one `REY_SECONDS` round, `hill` seconds held alone counted by `hillTick` once per second — by the bot host or big screen, anyone if stale — first to `HILL_TARGET`; the fallen get `downAt` and whoever runs them calls `respawnFighter` after `RESPAWN_MS`); `supervivencia` (people red, up to `SURVIVAL_MAX`, vs waves of bots `bot{w}w{i}` added in `addWave` when the last one falls — fallen bots leave the roster, people heal and the fallen get up; bots wait untouchable until `waveAt`; ends when all people are down, score `wavesSurvived`). `awardOutcome` gives the ranking result per mode (teams 1st/2nd, FFA place by `ffaRanking`, survival place null).
- Maps (`lib/arenaMaps.ts`, pure; `meta.map` an id or "azar" → `pickMap` per match into `state.map`): coliseo, puente (a deck over a `chasm`, rails with gaps, stronger `pushMul`), patio (walls, walkways at 1.8 m with ramps, crates jumpable below `STEP_UP` + a jump), bosque (`terrain` heights, trunks and low rocks) and trampas (fire grates on a timer and a blade turning in the middle, `trapHits` from the round clock). Geometry is circles + boxes (`Block`, ramps, an underside `b`), mirrored through the middle; `supportAt`/`blockedAt`/`sightBlocked`/`solidAt` drive the fighter's ground, walls, line of sight and arrows. Bots route around walls with `routeStep` (Dijkstra over `map.nav`), jump crates and the blade, and `steerSafe` keeps their moves and dodges off the void and `danger`; on the bridge they kick rivals with the void behind. Falling off is the `fell` event → `fallOff`.
- Events (`meta.events`, always online): `roundEvents(map, seed, key, seconds)` — lluvia (circles `rainDrops`, damage on landing), armas (`eventItems`, dropped once by `dropEventItems`, guarded by `evDrop`), achica (`ringRadius`, round maps only), niebla (`fogAt`; bots see less) — all from the seed and the round clock, announced with a banner (`EVENT_INFO`). Map damage (traps, ring, rain) is written only by whoever runs that fighter (its phone, the bot host or the big screen) with `envHit`, once per source and tick (`state.env`), as a hit from `ENV` (its runner already felt it, so it doesn't `receive` it again); a KO counts for the last hitter in `state.by` within `FALL_CREDIT_MS`. `lib/arena3dMaps.ts` builds the maps' scenes (merged stone, instanced crates/trees/rocks), the traps and `GroundFx` (hill, ring of fire, rain circles); `ArenaScene.setWorld` feeds them every frame. The pure modules (`arenaRules`, `arenaSim`, `arenaMaps`) run in node, so bots can be simulated headless through every map and mode.
- Red vs blue (`lib/teams.ts`), `meta.size` 1–3 per team, `meta.wins` rounds to win (best of 3/5), `meta.bots` fills empty places, `meta.potions` lays potions on the floor (they come back `POTION_RESPAWN_MS` after being drunk, via `respawns` + `respawnItems`), `meta.regen` gives health back slowly (`regenerate`, called by whoever runs the fighter, after `REGEN_DELAY_MS` without a hit; `HitNote.t` is the hit time). The match copies `potions`/`regen` into the state. Teams are picked while waiting (`state.teams`, `joinArena` auto-assigns); `startMatch` fixes the `roster` (people + bots `bot0`…) for the match. Each round is last team standing; the `ROUND_SECONDS` timeout gives it to the team with more total health.
- `lib/arenaSim.ts` is pure: the fighter on a map (red at -x; `step(…, map)` takes ground, walls and pillars from it), `WEAPONS` (daga, espada, hacha, maza, lanza, arco, martillo, mangual; plus escudo in the other hand), `Fighter` (walk/sprint, jump, stamina, dodge with a dash nobody can hit, acts idle → windup → strike (+ a small lunge) → recover, combos chained during the recovery that alternate the swing side (`combo`), bow draw, block/feint, flinch and guard break in `receive`; `ACTS` is append-only since poses send its index), the `meleeStrikes`/`flyArrow` hit tests, `roundItems` (seeded, mirrored weapon spots of the map) and the bots' brain (`botInput`, with `BotMind.skill` lower for early waves). Yaw 0 faces -z.
- No authority: each phone runs its own `Fighter` and writes `arena/poses/{fighterId}` (~15 Hz, with the act and when it started so others animate it). The attacker decides hits from the poses it sees and writes them with `strike` (transaction on `arena/state`: hp, kills, dropped gear, round end); the target reacts to `hits[itself].n`. Arrows: the shooter flies them and decides the hit; others draw `arena/arrows` from the launch values. The bot host (the connected person with the lowest id, once presence is loaded) runs every bot and writes for them; bot poses aren't removed on disconnect, so the next host continues from them. People gone `ABSENT_MS` mid-round are `forfeit`ed. Health, gear, items and score only change by transaction guarded by `roundKey`.
- Combat depth (`ACTS` gained `kick` and `special`, appended): a block raised within `PARRY_WINDOW` of a melee blow is a perfect parry. The pose carries `bt` (when the block started; 0 if it can't parry, e.g. re-raised within `PARRY_REARM`), the attacker sees it in `meleeStrikes`/`specialStrikes`, its own fighter reels (`Fighter.parried`) and the target gets `HitNote.pr`. The kick (`kickStrikes`) ignores guards and knocks a raised one aside (`HitNote.g`), has a cooldown and can be dodged. The special meter `state.special` (kept through a match's rounds) fills inside `strike` (`SPECIAL_*` in lib/arena.ts) and is spent with `spendSpecial`; callers only pass `input.special` while it's full. The special depends on the weapon (`specialOf`: torbellino, terremoto, estocada, triple); the knight doesn't flinch during it. Pads count presses `k`/`s`. `HitNote`'s extra fields (`pr`, `kk`, `g`, `sp`, `pw`, `st`) are only written when they apply. Kick/special effects and sounds come from the poses (`ArenaScene.takeFx`), so every screen shows them.
- Online (`state.online`): public arenas `ARENA1…` (`enterOnline`, the lobby's «En línea» tab and «Jugar ya»), no waiting room. `nextOnlineMatch` (any present player, once `nextAt` passes after a match, or at once when the battle on now has no people left) picks a mode (equipos/todos/rey; alone, sometimes supervivencia) and a map at random with events on, splits the people present into two even teams (at least 2 vs 2, up to 3 vs 3; FFA 4–6 knights) and fills with bots named from `RIVAL_NAMES`, never labelled as bots (no «· bot» in the bars or results); latecomers watch until the next one; the results show a countdown instead of rematch buttons. They count as people for the ranking.

It draws a split screen (`renderSplit`, `splitPeople`/`splitRects`: 1–4 people, bots get no view): each view over the person's shoulder, looking where that person looks; own name tag hidden in its own view. Sounds in `lib/arenaAudio.ts` (synthesized). Controls (`components/arena/Controls.tsx`): touch joystick + drag to look + buttons, or pointer-lock mouse + WASD (the pointer is only captured while you can fight, so the round-end and results buttons stay clickable).
- Modeled knight (`lib/arena3dModel.ts`, `public/models/talo-knight/`): the rigged Talo knight GLB plus the helmet pieces (`casco-*.glb`, made in Meshy, `HELM_PIECES` places them on the `Head` bone), loaded once (`loadKnightModel`) for the lobby preview (`KnightPreview3D`, which plays the GLB's idle clip) and the battle. ## Gotchas

- RTDB silently drops `null`-valued keys and empty arrays/objects on write. Fields that may be null/empty may be *absent* at runtime — normalize on read as `subscribeRace` and memotest's `normalizeState` do.
- RTDB turns objects with numeric keys into arrays; key such maps with strings (memotest uses `c{index}`).
- Room codes are 6 characters and uppercased in each `[roomId]/page.tsx`; `params` is a Promise in this Next version.
